# "Price kya hai?" — documentary script

A 3-minute documentary-style film about Shelf-to-Shop, in the manner of a nature or science channel: slow
camera, real sound first, a calm narrator, facts on screen, the people in the shop telling their part in
their own words. Everything in the app is shown live on https://shop.shankhyaai.com; no AI output is
staged. It closes with what comes next: Shelf-to-Shop inside SocialPulse, shown as a plan, because that
connection is designed but not built yet.

**Title:** PRICE KYA HAI?
**Subtitle card:** A shelf, a phone, and a shop that finally has a catalog.
**Length:** 3:00 exactly, the upload limit (a 60-second vertical cut for Instagram Reels is listed at the end).
**Language:** English narration; the shopkeeper and customer speak Hindi, with burned-in English subtitles.

---

## How it should feel

- **Sound before words.** Every act opens on natural sound: the shutter rolling up, packets rustling, a
  pressure cooker two shops away, the WhatsApp ping. Let it breathe two or three seconds before the narrator.
- **Slow, close camera.** Push-ins on hands, price stickers, coins on the counter, the phone screen. Hold
  shots for 3–5 seconds. Handheld is fine; keep it steady and slow.
- **Narrator:** warm, low, unhurried, third person, present tense, about 130 words a minute. Never excited.
  The technology is a reliable helper, not a miracle: no "magic", no "revolutionary".
- **On-screen facts** as plain white text, bottom left, with the source in small type under each one.
- **Music:** a low drone with a single santoor or sitar line, entering in Act 2, lifting gently in Act 4 and
  resolving under the closing line.
  Royalty-free only (YouTube Audio Library or Pixabay Music).

## Interview with the shopkeeper

Do not script their answers. Ask, let them talk in their own words, and pick the best 5–6 seconds.
Questions (in Hindi):

1. "WhatsApp pe customer aapse kya poochhte hain?" (What do customers ask you on WhatsApp?)
2. "Kabhi catalog banane ki koshish ki? Kya hua?" (Did you ever try to make a catalog? What happened?)
3. "Ek din mein kitne price wale message aate hain?" (How many price messages a day?)
4. After they try the app: "Pehli baar video se dukaan bani, kaisa laga?" (How did it feel to see the shop made from your video?)

Use only what they actually say. If an answer does not fit, cut the interview rather than ask them to read
a line.

## Before filming

- **Consent:** written permission from the shopkeeper, and from anyone whose face or voice is in the film.
  Film customers only with their permission, otherwise from behind or hands only. This matches the app's
  own rule: no customers in shelf videos.
- **Honesty:** show the app's real output. If processing takes two minutes, cut on the narrator's line
  instead of speeding up the screen or faking results. The SocialPulse part is a plan: say "will", show
  the card, and keep SocialPulse's screens exactly as they are.
- **Phone settings:** 1080p or 4K at 30 fps, lock exposure on the shelf, clean the lens.
- **Screen recordings:** browser zoom 110%, notifications off, the sample shop or your own shop.

## Recording the narration

Quiet room with soft furnishings (a wardrobe full of clothes works), phone 15–20 cm from the mouth, read
slowly, pause at every full stop. Record each line three times and keep the calmest.

**Narration, in order (about 290 words, about 135 seconds of speech):**

1. Every morning, in millions of small shops across India, the first question of the day arrives the same way.
2. India has more than seven crore small and micro businesses. Almost all of them already sell on WhatsApp.
3. A catalog would answer them all. But it means a photo, a name and a price, typed in for every item. In a shop with two hundred items, that is a day nobody has.
4. Shelf-to-Shop asks for one thing: walk past the shelf and say the prices, in Hindi, English, or both, the way you would to a customer.
5. Four of Meta's models do the reading.
6. Muse Spark watches and listens at the same time, finding each item and pairing it with the price spoken while it was on screen.
7. SAM 3.1 cuts each item out. Muse Image paints a clean background, while every pixel of the product stays exactly as filmed.
8. Voice Transcribe hears the numbers, even "ek hazaar do sau".
9. And when it is not sure, it does not guess. It asks.
10. Every price keeps its proof: the words that were said, and the second of the video they were said in.
11. The customer installs nothing. A link opens the shop.
12. One tap, and the whole order arrives in the shop's WhatsApp, numbered and added up.
13. When Diwali comes, one screen runs the sale, and a ready message goes out through the shopkeeper's own WhatsApp.
14. On our test videos, it found every item, and never published a wrong price without asking first.
15. What comes next is already designed. Inside SocialPulse, its assistant Phoenix will start a catalog from a single chat, and the shopkeeper will still approve every price before anything goes live.
16. The shelf was always the catalog. Now it can be read.

## Editing

- Free editors: DaVinci Resolve or CapCut. 16:9, 1080p. Check the final length is 3:00 or less.
- Colour: slightly warm, gentle contrast; keep screen recordings unfiltered so they stay readable.
- Subtitles burned in for all Hindi speech and the spoken prices.
- Check the facts on screen against `README.md` (sources) and `evals/report.md` before export.
- If the cut runs long, trim in this order: the Commerce Manager shot, the "What we heard" shot, the
  interview (to 4 seconds). Keep the cold open, the question being answered, the WhatsApp order and the
  SocialPulse epilogue.
